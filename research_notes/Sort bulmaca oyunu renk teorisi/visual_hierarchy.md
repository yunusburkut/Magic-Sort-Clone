# Color theory and visual hierarchy for a liquid-sort puzzle scene (background, glass, UI, VFX)

Scope: scene composition for a casual 2D mobile sort puzzle where 8-12 saturated liquid colors inside clear glass tubes are the gameplay focus, on a single solid or gradient background with no texture. Notes written 2026-10-21.

How to read these notes:
- "Cited Findings" are things a source actually says. Quality is flagged (primary standard, practitioner article, aggregator/secondary).
- "Inferences" include the researcher's own calculations. All contrast ratios in the Inferences were computed by the researcher with the WCAG 2.x relative-luminance formula and the OKLab/OKLCH conversion (Björn Ottosson's matrices). The sample liquid palette used for the calculations is a generic Material-like 10-color set, not the game's actual palette: red #E53935, orange #FB8C00, yellow #FDD835, lime #C0CA33, green #43A047, teal #00ACC1, blue #1E88E5, purple #8E24AA, pink #EC407A, brown #6D4C41. In OKLCH these sit at L 0.45-0.89 (most at 0.61-0.89) and C 0.12-0.21 (brown 0.05).
- Repo facts used in inferences: `Assets/HeronCaseRepo/Scripts/Data/GameSettings.cs` sets `_hiddenLiquidColor = (0.0566, 0.0566, 0.0566)` (about #0E0E0E) and `_outlineColor = (1, 0.85, 0.3)` (about #FFD94D, a warm gold) for the selection outline.

## Q1. How much should the background be lowered in chroma and separated in lightness from the gameplay pieces?

### Takeaway
Every art-direction source agrees on the principle (the background is pushed back in value and saturation; gameplay pieces own the highest contrast and saturation), but none of the game-art sources gives a number. The usable numbers come from UI/accessibility standards: WCAG 1.4.11 asks for at least 3:1 between a meaningful graphic and its adjacent color, and Material's HCT system says a tone (L*) gap of 40 guarantees at least 3:1 and a gap of 50 guarantees at least 4.5:1. Applied to a liquid palette whose lightness sits mostly at OKLCH L 0.6-0.9, that pushes the background to roughly L 0.2-0.3 (dark) with chroma kept around 0.02-0.06.

### Cited Findings
- Values should be planned at the scene level. Gameplay elements (heroes/units) should be the brightest, highest-contrast areas, and ground or background detail should use a small value range so it doesn't pull focus. "Contrast draws attention, and if everything is drawing attention, nothing will." — [Game Developer, "How to reduce visual confusion in your game"](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/how-to-reduce-visual-confusion-in-your-game) (practitioner case study, qualitative, no numbers)
- The grayscale test: desaturate a screenshot (in Photoshop, a black fill layer in Saturation blend mode above the image) and check whether it can be read at once. The DIGHARD example failed because the background, enemies and walkable surfaces were "all about the same value, the same shade of grey". It was fixed only by changing lights and darks (darker background, brighter play surfaces and character). — [Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/how-to-reduce-visual-confusion-in-your-game)
- The scheme can be inverted. Limbo uses light values in the back and very dark values for gameplay. What matters is separation, not "dark background" as such. — [Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/how-to-reduce-visual-confusion-in-your-game)
- Concept artists often desaturate background elements so key gameplay areas stand out. Backgrounds should be pushed back in value and saturation before detail is added. — search-result summaries of [Pixune, "Game Art Color Theory"](https://pixune.com/blog/color-theory-in-game-art-basics-and-complementary/) and [bugnet.io, "Readable game art"](https://bugnet.io/blog/readable-game-art-why-clarity-beats-detail) (secondary/blog. The bugnet URL returned only a blog index when fetched, so this rests on the search summary)
- Hyper-casual guidance from a publisher: "Heightening the contrast between the character and environment ensures they stand out and improves clarity of gameplay." Their examples are a bright track with a blue character, and a blue track with a bright-yellow stickman. They report a UI and contrast redesign in one A/B test raised D7 retention by 20%. — [Supersonic (Unity/ironSource) blog](https://supersonic.com/learn/blog/improve-the-clarity-of-your-hyper-casual-prototype-with-these-3-art-design-tips/) (publisher blog. The A/B numbers belong to one title and cover the whole UI redesign, not color alone)
- WCAG 2.2 SC 1.4.11 Non-text Contrast (AA): "The visual presentation of the following have a contrast ratio of at least 3:1 against adjacent color(s)": UI components and states, and "Parts of graphics required to understand the content". Values are not rounded: "2.999:1 would not meet the 3:1 threshold". — [W3C, Understanding SC 1.4.11](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/non-text-contrast.html) (primary standard. It does not mention games)
- Material HCT: "A difference of 40 in HCT tone guarantees a contrast ratio >= 3.0, and a difference of 50 guarantees a contrast ratio >= 4.5." Tone is CIELAB L* (0-100), which links the color system to contrast. — [Material color utilities HCT source (via Android Developers)](https://developer.android.com/agents/skills/xr/display-glasses-with-jetpack-compose-glimmer/references/material-hct-source) (primary, Google). The Material blog post that ties the 40/50 gaps to large and small elements was seen only as a search summary: [Material Design, "The science of color & design"](https://material.io/blog/science-of-color-design) (the page is JS-rendered and could not be fetched)
- A third-party design system extends the rule: a tone gap of 70 gives about 7:1. It uses the 40 gap for large text and secondary UI and the 50 gap for essential text and interactive elements. — search summary of [Dubai Design System, colors](https://designsystem.dubai.ae/foundations/colors) (secondary)
- Riot's VFX style guide (as summarized): primary elements get a "high value range and strong contrast in value or saturation" with high opacity and strong shape, and secondary elements sit in a lower value range. Higher saturation and a wider value range draw more focus. — [VFX Apprentice, "10 Design Tips from the League of Legends VFX Style Guide"](https://www.vfxapprentice.com/blog/10-league-of-legends-vfx-design-tips) (secondary summary of Riot's guide. The original PDF at nexus.leagueoflegends.com now redirects to the LoL homepage)

### Inferences
- A practical numeric rule (researcher's synthesis, not a published game-art standard). Treat each liquid's edge against the background as a WCAG 1.4.11 "graphical object required to understand the content". Aim for at least 3:1 for every liquid color against the background, which in HCT terms is a tone gap of 40 or more. Aim for 4.5:1 or more (a tone gap of 50 or more) for the most important pieces where you can.
- Computed for the sample palette (WCAG ratio, liquid vs background):
  - Dark indigo oklch(0.22 0.04 280) = #17182D: red 4.12, orange 7.35, yellow 12.49, lime 9.74, green 5.28, teal 6.37, blue 4.74, pink 4.63, **purple 2.48, brown 2.29**. White text on it: 17.4:1.
  - Dark violet oklch(0.30 0.07 300) = #33244B: lowest are purple 2.00 and brown 1.85. Red 3.32, blue 3.82 and pink 3.73 all drop below 4.5. A more saturated, lighter background costs contrast fast.
  - Mid slate oklch(0.45 0.03 260) = #4C5666: minimum 1.03 (brown). Mid neutral gray oklch(0.60 0 0) = #808080: minimum 1.05 (pink), maximum only 2.83 (yellow). **Mid-lightness backgrounds fail almost every vivid hue**, because vivid sRGB hues cluster at OKLCH L 0.6-0.9.
  - Light pastel oklch(0.93 0.025 250) = #DCEAF8: yellow 1.14, and the minimum OKLCH lightness gap is only 0.04.
  - Off-white oklch(0.97 0.01 90) = #F8F5EE: yellow 1.28, lime 1.64, orange 2.18, teal 2.51. Purple 6.47 and brown 6.99 do well.
- So "how much separation": with a typical vivid palette, a dark background at OKLCH L ≈ 0.18-0.26 (about L* 15-25) gives a gap of about 0.35-0.7 to the bright and mid liquids and clears 3:1 for all hues except the inherently dark ones (purple, brown, navy, dark green). Those need either a lighter recipe (purple at L ≥ 0.55-0.60) or a bright glass rim or outline to carry the 3:1 edge.
- Keep background chroma about 0.02-0.06 in OKLCH. The violet example shows that raising chroma and lightness together (0.30/0.07) pulls three more liquids under 4.5:1. This range is the researcher's estimate from the calculation, not from a source.
- The Helmholtz-Kohlrausch effect (see Q2) means saturated reds, pinks, blues and purples will *look* brighter against a dark background than their WCAG ratio suggests. Yellow and green get less of this boost. WCAG ratios are therefore conservative for the cool and red liquids on dark backgrounds, and a liquid that measures 2.5:1 may look acceptable. It should still be checked in grayscale.
- Grayscale check: in a desaturated screenshot, every tube and its liquid layers should separate from the background at a glance (the Game Developer test). Also run a check for color-vision deficiency (see Q4).

### Gaps
- No game-art source (King, Playrix, Supercell, Grand Games, GDC) found publishes a numeric luminance or chroma gap between pieces and background. The 3:1 / tone-40 rule is borrowed from UI accessibility standards and is not established game-art practice.
- James Gurney's *Color and Light* (atmospheric perspective, "color accents" against grayed fields) was not accessible online. Only secondary pages about his gamut-masking method were reached.
- Apple HIG Color and the Material 3 color-roles pages are JS-rendered and could not be fetched, so their exact wording is not quoted here.

## Q2. Which background best supports 8-12 vivid hues: desaturated dark blue/indigo/purple, near-neutral, or light pastel?

### Takeaway
For a full-gamut liquid palette, the safest choice is a **dark, low-chroma, cool near-neutral** (a desaturated navy, indigo or slate at OKLCH L ≈ 0.18-0.28, C ≈ 0.02-0.05). It is the only option where most vivid hues clear 3:1, and it keeps hue competition small. A mid-value background is the worst choice. A light pastel or off-white kills yellows, limes and oranges, which a sort game cannot drop. Any hue in the background competes with the matching liquid and tints the clear glass, so keep background chroma low whatever the hue. Data-visualization guidance prefers light neutral backgrounds for categorical palettes, but that assumes a palette designed for white.

### Cited Findings
- Simultaneous contrast: "a strip of grey on a brightly coloured field appears to be tinted ever so slightly in the contrasting colour" (Müller, 1838). Grey looks slightly reddish on green and greenish on red. Lightness contrast: "a grey image on a black background appears much brighter than the same on white" (Goethe). — [Wikipedia, "Contrast effect"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contrast_effect) (encyclopedic. The primary sources are Chevreul and Albers, which were not reachable)
- Albers' thesis (via summaries): color is context-dependent, and identical patches look lighter or darker, or shifted in hue, on different grounds. — search results for *Interaction of Color* summaries (low-quality PDF mirrors, so treat as background knowledge only). No authoritative Albers page was reached.
- Helmholtz-Kohlrausch effect: "the intense saturation of spectral hue is perceived as part of the color's luminance". Highly saturated reds, pinks and blues show the effect most strongly. "Two colors that do not have as great of an Helmholtz–Kohlrausch effect as the others are green and yellow." — [Wikipedia, "Helmholtz–Kohlrausch effect"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helmholtz%E2%80%93Kohlrausch_effect)
- Categorical palettes (data-viz analog for "many distinct hues"): keep about 6-12 categories at most. Make adjacent colors differ in *lightness*, not only hue, so charts "will still work even if all colour is completely stripped away". Avoid red-green reliance. Each color must reach at least 3:1 against the background. "Avoid textured, patterned or dark backgrounds, as they reduce the intended colour contrast." — [NHS Scotland Turas design system, categorical palette](https://www.turasdesignsystem.nhs.scot/design/data-visualisation/categorical-palette/) (primary design-system guidance. Note that its palette was *designed for* white #FFFFFF and #F7F8FD)
- Most data should be neutral gray, with bright color kept for what matters. A neutral ground lets the pattern show without color getting in the way. — search summaries of [Visualising Data, "Make grey your best friend"](https://visualisingdata.com/2015/01/make-grey-best-friend/) and [Towards Data Science, "8 rules for optimal use of color in data visualization"](https://towardsdatascience.com/8-rules-for-optimal-use-of-color-in-data-visualization-b283ae1fc1e2/) (secondary)
- Dark surfaces: Material's baseline dark theme uses "dark grey instead of black" for background and surface colors. Surfaces get "lighter and more colorful at higher elevations". — [Material Components for Android, Dark theme docs](https://github.com/material-components/material-components-android/blob/master/docs/theming/Dark.md) (primary). The #121212 value and the advice to desaturate accents on dark surfaces come only from secondary write-ups. Saturated colors "vibrate" or cause "halation (glow/bleed) against dark backgrounds", so tone down saturation. Tint the base surface toward a brand hue at very low saturation (2-5%). Use off-white text such as #E1E1E1 rather than pure white. — [Medium, "Designing effective dark mode interfaces"](https://medium.com/@tundehercules/designing-effective-dark-mode-interfaces-17f38ecea2e9) and [vibehackers dark-mode skill page](https://vibehackers.io/claude-code/skills/dark-mode-sickn33) (secondary/low authority. The official Material dark-theme page could not be fetched)
- OKLCH practice in current design systems (search summaries, unverified per page): near-black bases at about oklch(0.145 0 0) to oklch(0.22 ...), with cards one step lighter (about 0.205). Very light and very dark steps need *less* chroma than mid steps to stay in gamut. Vivid accents sit at about L 0.55-0.70 with C ≥ 0.2. — [atmos.style OKLCH glossary](https://atmos.style/glossary/oklch-color-space), [LuminescentDev color system (Mintlify)](https://www.mintlify.com/LuminescentDev/ui/concepts/color-system) (aggregated search results. The search tool flagged the specific numbers as its own synthesis, so do not treat them as a standard)
- Casual-game palette commentary: backgrounds should set mood "without overpowering foreground elements", and interactive elements need strong contrast. — [itch.io, "Picking the Perfect Color Palette for Your Game"](https://itch.io/blog/1039646/picking-the-perfect-color-palette-for-your-game). Casual art is bright and clean, high-contrast for clarity, with restraint to avoid visual fatigue. — [Pixune, "What Is Casual Game Art?"](https://pixune.com/blog/casual-game-art/) (vendor blogs, opinion). One palette roundup shows a pastel casual example on a deep violet background. — [DesignYourWay, gaming color palettes](https://www.designyourway.net/blog/gaming-color-palettes/) (listicle)
- Genre observation: water-sort store listings sell "colorful backgrounds that soothe your eyes" and many background themes. A university project version of the genre used a watery background whose color changes subtly each level. — [Univ. of Tartu CG course project "Glittery Water Puzzle"](https://courses.cs.ut.ee/2023/cg/fall/Main/Project-GlitteryWaterPuzzle), [App Store listing example](https://apps.apple.com/US/app/id6466972408) (marketing copy, not design guidance)

### Inferences
- **Dark, desaturated cool background (recommended for this game).** From the calculation in Q1: at oklch(0.22 0.04 280) 8 of 10 sample hues clear 4:1 and the bright half clear 6:1. Only the deliberately dark liquids (purple, brown) fall to about 2.3-2.5:1. It also gives the most headroom for white or gold UI text and white glass highlights (white on #17182D = 17.4:1). Cool hues (blue, indigo, slate) are the conventional "receding" choice, and a cool cast at C ≤ 0.05 is close enough to neutral that it barely competes with blue or purple liquids. Lightness separation, not hue, does the work.
- **Why not saturated purple or indigo.** At C ≈ 0.07 and L 0.30, red, blue and pink fall below 4:1, and purple and brown liquids drop to about 2:1. By simultaneous contrast, a strongly violet field also pushes adjacent clear glass and white highlights slightly toward yellow-green and visibly mutes the blue and purple liquids. Keep any hue in the background "felt, not seen".
- **Why not mid gray or mid slate.** Minimum contrast is about 1.03-1.05:1. Several liquids would merge with the background in grayscale, failing the Game Developer value test outright.
- **Why not light pastel or off-white for this genre.** Yellow (1.14-1.28:1), lime (1.6:1) and orange (2.2:1) almost disappear, and clear glass has nothing to define it except dark outlines (see Q6). A light background can work only if (a) every tube has a dark rim at ≥ 3:1, and (b) the liquid palette is rebuilt so its lightest hue is about L ≤ 0.75. That throws away the bright lemon yellow and lime that sort games usually need. The Turas advice to prefer light backgrounds assumes a palette tuned to white, so it does not carry over to a full-brightness game palette.
- **Hue choice for the background.** Choose the hue that the liquid palette is *least* sensitive to. Usually that is a blue-violet near-neutral (around hue 260-285), because the dark blue and purple liquids can be raised in lightness to separate. Warm dark backgrounds (brown, plum) clash with the brown, red, orange and pink liquids and make the glass look dirty. Complementary pairing (for example a teal-tinted ground behind mostly warm pieces) only works when the piece palette is one-sided. With 8-12 hues spanning the wheel there is no complement to all of them, which is why near-neutral wins.
- **Halation caveat.** Avoid pure black (#000). Stay at L ≈ 0.18-0.26 with a faint tint. Expect the most saturated reds, magentas and blues to "glow" on dark backgrounds (H-K effect). If they look like they bleed, give the glass a thin light rim, rather than desaturating the liquids, which are the gameplay focus.
- **Repo-specific risk.** The hidden-liquid color in GameSettings.cs (about #0E0E0E) measures 1.11:1 against #17182D and 1.32:1 against #1A2941. On any dark background a hidden segment would read as an empty hole, not as "unknown liquid". On a dark scene it should be a mid-dark neutral (about oklch(0.38-0.45 0 0)) with a "?" mark or pattern, or a lighter frosted fill.

### Gaps
- No published comparison (A/B or survey) of dark vs light backgrounds in sort or match puzzle games was found. Popular titles use both. Commercial practice could not be verified from studio sources (King, Playrix, Grand Games published nothing on this topic in the results).
- No primary source was found for the claim that cool colors recede and warm colors advance (common painting teaching, for example Gurney on atmospheric perspective). It is used above as a convention only.

## Q3. How do artists use gradients, vignettes and subtle radial light to direct attention without texture or noise?

### Takeaway
The technique is well established in photography and illustration: brighten or keep the area around the focal point, darken toward the edges with a heavily feathered radial gradient, and never let the edge of the gradient show. No game-specific numeric standard was found. For a no-texture background, use a *value-only* radial or vertical gradient within a narrow lightness band, and add dithering or a higher color depth so smooth gradients don't band.

### Cited Findings
- A radial gradient can be placed anywhere to pull attention to an off-center subject. Keep the inside untouched and apply darkening outside. You can stack a second, brightening gradient inside the focal area, and adjust saturation the same way. "Use the Feather slider to control the sharpness of the edge of the gradient. Make sure that the edge is not noticeable." — [Adobe Lightroom tutorial, "Photo vignette"](https://www.adobe.com/learn/lightroom-cc/web/photo-vignette) (primary vendor tutorial, photography)
- A vignette implemented as a mostly transparent radial gradient over the scene darkens the edges. Use canvas-space (userSpaceOnUse) coordinates so it aligns to the whole screen rather than an object. — [Hyring, "SVG Radial Gradient"](https://hyring.com/learn/svg/radial-gradient) (tutorial)
- Value planning beats texture: ground and background areas should use a narrow value range so they don't pull focus. — [Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/how-to-reduce-visual-confusion-in-your-game)
- Excessive glow and particles hurt readability (and GPU performance) in mobile games. — [Yudiz, "Mobile Game Art Styles 2026"](https://www.yudiz.com/insights/mobile-game-art-styles/) (vendor blog, opinion)
- Hyper-casual layout: put the playable zone in roughly the lower third of the screen, where the thumb operates. — [Supersonic](https://supersonic.com/learn/blog/improve-the-clarity-of-your-hyper-casual-prototype-with-these-3-art-design-tips/) (runner/stickman context. A sort game's tubes usually fill the middle band)

### Inferences
- Recommended recipe (researcher's synthesis):
  1. Base: one dark, low-chroma cool color (see Q2).
  2. Radial "spotlight": center it on the tube cluster, not the screen center. The center is about +0.04 to +0.08 OKLCH L above the edges (for example L 0.27 at the center to L 0.19 at the corners). Keep the same hue and the same or slightly lower chroma toward the edges. The radius should cover the whole tube area so no tube sits on the falloff.
  3. Optional vertical gradient: top slightly lighter or cooler than the bottom (or the reverse), again within about 0.05-0.08 L. Large L swings put tubes at the top and bottom on different contrast backgrounds and break consistent readability.
  4. Edges: the vignette darkens the corners by a further ~0.03-0.06 L. Feather it fully so no ring is visible.
  5. Keep the brightest background point at least ~0.35 L below the darkest liquid you need to read. If dark liquids are used, keep the spotlight subtle so it doesn't cut their contrast.
- Banding: smooth dark gradients on 8-bit sRGB band visibly (about 0.004 per step near black). Add very low-amplitude dither (±0.5-1 LSB, blue-noise or ordered) in the background shader, or render the gradient at higher precision. This adds no visible texture. It is a technical inference, not sourced here.
- A gradient may shift *hue* slightly (for example navy toward indigo) but should not cross into a hue family that matches a liquid at high chroma.
- Light motion (a slow breathing of the spotlight) should be no more than a few percent L, so it doesn't compete with pour animation.

### Gaps
- No GDC talk or casual-studio write-up gives numeric vignette strength or radial-gradient ranges for mobile puzzle games. The numbers above are the researcher's estimates and should be tested on device.

## Q4. How should UI text, buttons and accents be colored relative to the background and the liquid palette (WCAG 2.2, 60-30-10, harmony)?

### Takeaway
Use WCAG 2.2 as the floor: body and small UI text at 4.5:1 or more (AAA 7:1), large text (18 pt, or 14 pt bold) and UI components or icons at 3:1 or more, computed without rounding. On a dark low-chroma background, near-white text easily reaches AAA. Treat the liquid palette as the scene's "accent" (the 10% in 60-30-10) and keep UI chrome to 1-2 colors. Pick one UI accent whose hue and role don't double as a liquid color (or make it clearly a different material, such as a gold or white button with a dark outline), and keep the call-to-action button the biggest and brightest UI element.

### Cited Findings
- WCAG 2.2 SC 1.4.3 Contrast (Minimum, AA): text and images of text at least 4.5:1. Large-scale text at least 3:1. Large-scale is "at least 18 point or 14 point bold". Logotypes and purely decorative text are exempt. 4.5:1 compensates for vision loss equivalent to about 20/40. SC 1.4.6 (AAA) uses 7:1 (about 20/80). — [W3C, Understanding SC 1.4.3](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/contrast-minimum.html) (primary standard)
- SC 1.4.11 (AA): UI components, their states and focus indicators need 3:1 against adjacent colors. Inactive components are exempt. — [W3C, Understanding SC 1.4.11](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/non-text-contrast.html)
- Large-text pixel equivalents commonly given: 24 px regular, or 18.66 px bold. — search summary of [WebAIM, contrast](https://webaim.org/articles/contrast/) and [Stark, WCAG explained](https://www.getstark.co/wcag-explained/perceivable/distinguishable/contrast-minimum/) (secondary, consistent with W3C)
- HCT tone gaps: 40 gives ≥ 3:1, 50 gives ≥ 4.5:1 (see Q1), so a UI palette built in tone steps can be checked without measuring. — [Material HCT source](https://developer.android.com/agents/skills/xr/display-glasses-with-jetpack-compose-glimmer/references/material-hct-source)
- 60-30-10 comes from interior design. About 60% is a base or neutral covering large surfaces, 30% a secondary for mid-level components, and 10% an accent for CTAs and highlights. Two colors can read flat, and four or more compete and blur the hierarchy. Working in grayscale first is advised. — [UX Planet, "The 60–30–10 Rule"](https://uxplanet.org/the-60-30-10-rule-a-foolproof-way-to-choose-colors-for-your-ui-design-d15625e56d25), [Pixpa, "60 30 10 rule in web design"](https://www.pixpa.com/blog/60-30-10-rule-in-web-design) (design-practice heuristic, opinion. No game-specific source found)
- Hyper-casual UI: menu screens should use 1-2 colors "to avoid cognitive overload", have at most 3-4 buttons, and make the start button "the biggest and brightest". Red is read as danger. — [Supersonic](https://supersonic.com/learn/blog/improve-the-clarity-of-your-hyper-casual-prototype-with-these-3-art-design-tips/)
- Harmony through restriction: Gurney's gamut masking describes a color scheme as a shape cut out of the color wheel, and harmony comes from *leaving colors out*. Krita ships preset masks such as "Atmospheric Triad" and "Complementary". — [Lines and Colors, "James Gurney on gamut masking"](https://linesandcolors.com/2011/09/19/james-gurney-on-gamut-masking/), [Krita manual, Gamut Masks](https://docs.krita.org/user_manual/gamut_masks.html) (secondary. Gurney's own posts were not fetched)
- Don't put text directly on palette colors. Put it on a neutral background. Don't rely on hue alone. — [Turas categorical palette](https://www.turasdesignsystem.nhs.scot/design/data-visualisation/categorical-palette/). Gameplay information should not depend on hue alone, because it is lost for players with color-vision deficiency. — search summary of [bugnet.io](https://bugnet.io/blog/readable-game-art-why-clarity-beats-detail) (secondary)

### Inferences
- **60-30-10 adapted to a sort puzzle** (researcher's mapping, opinion):
  - About 60%: background (dark low-chroma cool), the visual "silence".
  - About 30%: neutral structure. Glass tubes (mostly transparent, with white or very light highlights), the HUD bar, panels. These are near-neutral or the background hue lifted by about 0.08-0.15 L. They must not introduce a new saturated hue.
  - About 10%: saturated color. This is **the liquids themselves** plus at most one UI accent used sparingly (CTA button, coins, stars). Because the liquids already use the full wheel, the UI accent budget is close to zero. Use it only on the primary CTA and the win screen.
  - Harmony scheme: the liquids are a "full-wheel" set by necessity. Harmony comes from everything *else* being in a narrow gamut mask: one cool background hue family, plus neutral glass, plus a single warm accent (gold or amber) for reward and CTA. In Gurney's terms the scene is an atmospheric mask with one complementary accent, and the liquids are the exception that gets full saturation.
- **Text colors on a dark background** (computed): white on #17182D = 17.4:1 (AAA). An off-white such as oklch(0.95 0.01 260) is still well above 7:1 and less harsh. Secondary or caption text at about oklch(0.75-0.80 0.02 260) still clears 7:1 against L 0.22. Title text can be a light warm gold (L ≥ 0.85), which clears 4.5:1 easily on L ≤ 0.25. Add a dark outline or drop shadow if it overlaps tubes or VFX.
- **Buttons:** the CTA fill should be the brightest, most saturated non-liquid element (for example a gold-to-amber or green gradient at L 0.70-0.85). Its label must reach 4.5:1 against the fill (dark text on light-gold fills, or white text with a dark stroke on mid-L fills). The button edge must reach 3:1 against the background (1.4.11). Secondary buttons: neutral or ghost (outline only, light at L ≥ 0.75 on dark).
- **Avoid hue collisions:** don't make a UI accent the same hue *and* lightness as a liquid (for example a lemon-yellow UI glow and a lemon-yellow liquid). Differentiate by material (metallic gradient, white rim, icon) or by lightness.
- **Color-vision deficiency:** the 8-12 liquid set should differ in lightness between commonly confused pairs (red/green, blue/purple, orange/yellow). A colorblind mode with symbols on liquid segments is common in the genre (no source fetched for the genre specifically).

## Q5. Which colors work for celebration and feedback VFX (confetti, sparkle, splash, selection glow, solved-tube glow) alongside a multi-hue palette?

### Takeaway
Riot's VFX guidance (via summary) gives the useful rules. Feedback that must read gets the widest value range, strong value or saturation contrast and high opacity. Supporting elements sit lower in value. Avoid 0% and 100% saturation and value so effects don't merge with the environment or UI. For a full-wheel liquid palette, that points to **lightness-led feedback**: near-white cores with a warm-gold or tube-colored halo. Multi-color confetti fights the liquids, and sparkles that read by brightness rather than hue work better. No source gives gold or white numbers for reward screens. That part is inference.

### Cited Findings
- Primary VFX elements use a high value range, strong value or saturation contrast, high opacity and strong shape. Secondary elements use lower value ranges and support the primary. "Secondary elements should use color values and saturation to enhance primary elements." "Steer clear from using 100% or 0% saturation" to avoid blending into the environment or UI. A wider value range and glow draw more focus, but check whether the glow helps or distracts. Contrast "only works if there is a dominant color in the effect". — [VFX Apprentice summary of Riot's LoL VFX Style Guide](https://www.vfxapprentice.com/blog/10-league-of-legends-vfx-design-tips) (secondary summary of a primary studio guide)
- A devlog reports that, after their 2D artist rebalanced colors, swapping confetti for "sparkling particles" worked much better on a creature-reveal reward screen. — [Picking Pies, Archeonimals devlog, "How to improve a 'new creature' screen"](https://pickingpies.itch.io/archeonimals-tap-dig-collect/devlog/113518/how-to-improve-a-new-creature-screen) (single developer anecdote)
- Juice includes confetti on level complete, coin-collect pops and screen shake. Feedback in roughly a 60-150 ms band feels responsive but stays readable. Amplified feedback should be reserved for successful actions so it keeps its meaning. — search summaries of [itch.io, "Making a game feel juicy with simple effects"](https://itch.io/blog/1059831/making-a-game-feel-juicy-with-simple-effects) and [DEV, "The reward moment is the product"](https://dev.to/patilrb/the-reward-moment-is-the-product-43h6) (blogs, opinion)
- Excessive glow and particles hurt readability on mobile. — [Yudiz](https://www.yudiz.com/insights/mobile-game-art-styles/)

### Inferences
- **Selection glow (tube lifted or selected).** Its job is "this tube is picked", independent of the liquid colors. Best practice by the Riot logic is a lightness cue: a white or very light cool rim (L ≥ 0.9, C ≤ 0.05) plus the lift motion. **Repo check:** the current gold outline (about #FFD94D) measures 1.02:1 against a lemon-yellow liquid (#FDD835). Where the outline touches or overlaps a yellow, orange or lime top layer, the selection edge vanishes, and it uses the same hue family as the reward and CTA accent. Consider white or near-white with a soft outer glow, or keep gold but make it thicker and add a white inner line.
- **Solved-tube glow.** Use the *tube's own liquid color* for the halo (it says "this color is done"), with a white core or rim so it reads on the dark background whatever the hue. Keep the halo at low opacity (about 25-50%) and soft, so it doesn't spill onto neighboring tubes and change their perceived hue (simultaneous contrast, Q2). The cap snap plus a brief white flash carries the "done" beat.
- **Pour splash and droplets.** Use the poured liquid's color, lightened slightly (+0.05-0.1 L) at the crest, with white specular dots. Don't add other hues: a splash is information about *which* liquid moved.
- **Sparkles.** White to pale warm gold (about oklch(0.92-0.97 0.04-0.10 85-95)), small, high opacity, short life. They read by brightness, not hue, so they never "match" a liquid. Reserve the strongest, near-white values for the moment of success (Riot: the widest value range for the most important effect).
- **Confetti on the win screen.** Either (a) metallic gold, white and one background-family tint, or (b) the level's own liquid colors. Avoid generic rainbow confetti over the board while tubes are still visible, because it reintroduces every liquid hue as noise. Darken or dim the board (a 40-60% black or background-color scrim) under the win panel so confetti and title sit on a quiet field.
- **Win screen and title.** Warm gold title (L ≥ 0.85) with a dark outline, on a darkened scrim. Gold is the single warm accent, which pairs with the cool background as a near-complement (blue-violet and gold-amber sit roughly opposite on the hue wheel). This is a widely used convention, not a sourced rule.
- **Budget.** Keep at most one full-screen effect at a time. Celebration particles should not cover tubes during play. Hold particle saturation below the liquids' (avoid 100% saturation, per Riot) so the liquids stay the most saturated things on screen except at the win moment.

## Q6. How should transparent glass, its highlights and outlines be colored so it reads on the background?

### Takeaway
Glass has no color of its own. It is defined by edges, highlights and what it does to the background behind it. On a dark background the rim and specular highlights should be light (white or a background-tinted near-white) and kept sparse, with hard-edged streaks along the form, while the body stays mostly transparent at low opacity. The glass outline is the one element of the empty tube that must meet about 3:1 against the background so empty tubes stay readable.

### Cited Findings
- A glass edge is defined by value difference with the background. Against a dark backdrop the rim can read as a bright white line. Use "lost and found" edges (some edges fade into the background) rather than a constant outline. The curves of shadows and highlights create the illusion of transparency. — search summary of [ArtistsNetwork, "How to Draw Glass: Use Your Eyes, Not Your Memory"](https://www.artistsnetwork.com/art-techniques/drawing/how-to-draw-glass) (secondary)
- Stylized digital glass recipe: paint the glass body in a light color at low opacity (the author used **18%**, with lower opacity for more transparent glass). Blur the background behind it. Shadows go in dark blue at about **30% opacity, Multiply**, only in the thick parts (bottom, neck). Highlights are hard-edged long strokes that follow the form, plus small dots. **Avoid pure black and pure white**: use tinted light highlights and dark blue or purple darks. Sample reflection colors from the background. Add a very small white hard-edged highlight along edges and a soft light-colored glow around the glass (Soft Light or Add). — [Clip Studio Tips, "How to Draw GLASS"](https://tips.clip-studio.com/zh-tw/articles/3432?org=1) (illustration tutorial)
- 1.4.11 requires 3:1 for "parts of graphics required to understand the content". — [W3C 1.4.11](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/non-text-contrast.html)

### Inferences
- **Glass on a dark background** (recommended values, researcher's synthesis):
  - Body tint: near-white or background-hue light at about 6-15% opacity (Clip Studio's 18% is the upper end). The liquid shows through almost unchanged, and the empty part reads as a faint lighter column.
  - Rim and outline: light, about oklch(0.85-0.95 0.01-0.03 hue of the background) at about 50-80% opacity, 2-4 px at phone scale. Check that the *composited* rim color reaches ≥ 3:1 against the background (on #17182D any color above about L 0.55 does). Make the outer silhouette continuous at the bottom and sides. Lost-and-found edges suit illustration more than gameplay readability.
  - Speculars: one or two long vertical streaks (white, 60-90% opacity) on the light side, plus a small hard dot near the shoulder. Keep them narrow (about 8-15% of the tube width) so they don't hide liquid colors. Use white or very slightly cool white, not gold, so they don't compete with the reward accent.
  - Inner shading: a slightly darker band (background color, Multiply, about 20-30%) on the shadow side and at the bottom gives volume without adding hue.
  - Liquid inside glass: add a thin lighter line (a meniscus) at the liquid top and a subtle darker inner edge on the liquid sides. These help adjacent layers of similar lightness separate.
- **Glass on a light background (if chosen):** the white highlights disappear, so the outline must become dark (a background-hue dark at L ≤ 0.45 to reach 3:1 on L 0.95), and the glass reads more like a drawn container than clear glass.
- **Interaction with selection and solved states:** keep the glass rim neutral at rest so a state change (selection = brighter or white rim, solved = colored halo plus cap) is a clear delta from the rest state.
