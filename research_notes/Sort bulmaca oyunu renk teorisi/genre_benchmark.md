# Genre benchmark: color choices in top mobile color-sort puzzle games (water / liquid / ball sort)

Research date: 2026-10-21. Sources: App Store listings and reviews (fetched Oct 2026), GitHub issues, TechCrunch, a Substack analysis, search-engine summaries of store listings.

Method note: Every App Store and Google Play page was fetched as text. Google Play pages came back empty, and screenshot images were not readable as pixels. So **no hex values were sampled from any game in this session**. Wherever this file mentions specific hues or layouts that are not tied to a quoted source, they sit under "Inferences" and are labelled as unverified recollection or design reasoning.

---

## 1. What do the leading titles use for liquid colors, backgrounds, bottles and UI?

### Takeaway
Store copy from the leaders sells color as "vivid", "a riot of colors" or a "relaxing palette". None of them publish their palette, color count or hex values. The documented visual differentiators are theme (Magic Sort: wizard / potion / cat fantasy; IEC: "happy glass" plus seasonal events) and cosmetic skins (backgrounds, bottles or tubes, caps or stoppers). These skins are sold or unlocked in a shop. Exact liquid palettes remain unverified.

### Cited Findings
**Magic Sort! (Grand Games A.Ş.)**
- The US App Store listing shows 4.7 stars from 695K ratings, version 33.69 (updated the day before the fetch, Oct 2026). Every version note reads "New 100 levels! - Bug Fixes & Improvements". No version note mentions color or accessibility. — [App Store: Magic Sort!](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/magic-sort/id6499209744)
- How the description pitches the visuals: "A Mystical Water Sort Adventure", "Vivid Splashes: A riot of colors makes every move a delight, turning sorting into a visual feast". It also mentions "Wizard's Gear" tools, a cauldron where you "stir up boosts", and "Mystical Ingredients". — [App Store: Magic Sort!](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/magic-sort/id6499209744)
- Accessibility section: "The developer has not yet indicated which accessibility features this app supports." — [App Store: Magic Sort!](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/magic-sort/id6499209744)
- Reviews mention "little magician cats" and "fun little collectible cards". One reviewer says that on a retry "There are completely different colors and fill patterns for the bottles." Another says the player "can't see what colors are in the tubes", which makes later levels feel like luck. This points to hidden or mystery layers. A third says the hardest levels have "too many colors and too few bottles". — [App Store reviews: Magic Sort!](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/magic-sort/id6499209744?see-all=reviews)
- Commercial context: In Jan 2025 Grand Games raised a $30M Series A led by Balderton. Magic Sort had broken into Apple's top-selling casual charts. The studio "leans heavily on AI tools for some of the time-consuming work involved in artwork ideas". — [TechCrunch, 2025-01-09](https://techcrunch.com/2025/01/09/grand-games-the-turkish-gaming-startup-behind-pouring-game-magic-sort-drinks-up-30m/)
- A third-party estimate puts Magic Sort at about $120K in daily revenue (March 2025) and calls it the top sorting game by IAP. This was not verified. — [Algoryte Substack, 2025-03-22](https://algoryte.substack.com/p/the-ultimate-sorting-game-face-off)
- Conflicting rating counts: an app-comparison site (2026) lists about 326.5K reviews, while the App Store page fetched in Oct 2026 shows 695K ratings. They may count different things (reviews vs ratings) or come from different dates. — [AppPricingLab](https://www.apppricinglab.com/compare/apple/6499209744/6755425214) vs [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/magic-sort/id6499209744)
- Not to be confused with "Magic Sort: Bottle Fill Puzzle" (id1605670313), a different developer's game. — [App Store: Magic Sort: Bottle Fill Puzzle](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1605670313)

**Water Sort Puzzle (IEC GLOBAL PTY LTD)**
- Listing shows 4.7 stars from 324K ratings. The description calls the containers "glasses", not tubes: "Try to sort the watercolor in the glasses until all colors in the same happy glass". It cites "happy glass, waterpark sort, and magic sort" as inspirations, plus "Santa Claus and Christmas cheer" touches. Version 20.0.0 (2025-12-03) is "Update Chrismas Event". No version note mentions colors or accessibility. — [App Store: Water Sort Puzzle (IEC)](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/water-sort-puzzle/id1514542157)
- A 2023 review says "The background and bottles you start with aren't bad". It confirms that backgrounds and bottles can be bought with coins. The same review says "Every once and a while there are levels where you only see the color closet to the top", which describes hidden-layer levels. — [App Store reviews: Water Sort Puzzle (IEC)](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/water-sort-puzzle/id1514542157?see-all=reviews)

**Water Sort Puzzle: Get Color (Tripledot Studios)**
- Tripledot Studios is the developer, and the game is billed as a "3D Match & Pour Sorting Game". A third-party tracker lists the first release as Sep 8, 2021. — [App Store: Get Color](https://apps.apple.com/US/app/id1569776448); [mwm.ai tracker (unofficial)](https://mwm.ai/>/apps/water-sort-puzzle-get-color/1569776448)
- Reviews from 2024: "I already got all the bottles, wallpapers and colors. (There's not a lot of options)". This means liquid colors are themselves an unlockable cosmetic, alongside bottles and wallpapers. Another review asks to "change the tubes styles, liquid color, and background styles". The developer replied: "We appreciate your ideas about continuous progression and customization options." — [App Store reviews: Get Color](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/water-sort-puzzle-get-color/id1569776448?see-all=reviews)

**Water Sort Quest (MOBIRIX)**
- The description says "You can customize tubes, caps, and backgrounds" and "Red-green colorblind mode available". Version 1.0.5 "Added completion effect for stoppers". Version 1.3.1 "Restored skin customization for Beakers, Stoppers, and Backgrounds". — [App Store: Water Sort Quest](https://apps.apple.com/app/id6462847997)

**Other titles**
- "Water Sort puzzle - Color sort" (Essendon Studios): version 1.0.3 adds "new themes and environments" and "improved graphics". — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1582157018)
- "Water Sort Puzzle - Sort Color" (Play Store): an update adds new backgrounds and bottle styles. — [Google Play (search summary)](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=water.sort.puzzle.game.color.sorting.free&hl=en_IN)
- The open-source "Water Sort" (sidhant947, on F-Droid) describes "a relaxing color palette" and a "Minimalist design". — [GitHub: sidhant947/water-sort](https://github.com/sidhant947/water-sort)
- A Godot game-jam water sort (hemmond) lets players choose 3 to 15 colors. — [itch.io: hemmond water sort](https://hemmond-cz.itch.io/water-sort)

### Inferences
- **Skin layers are the genre norm.** The cosmetic stack is background (wallpaper), bottle or tube shape, cap or stopper, and sometimes the liquid palette itself. Get Color, IEC, Water Sort Quest and Essendon all show this (cited above). So the default palette and background have to read well on several skin combinations, not just one.
- **Hidden-layer levels add a visual state.** Magic Sort and IEC both have levels where lower layers are concealed (reviews above). Games need a "mystery" visual state that is clearly not a liquid color. The usual approach is a dark or neutral fill, often with a "?". The look of that state is not documented in the sources.
- **Unverified recollection (not checked this session):**
  - The classic IEC-style palette is about 10 to 12 flat, highly saturated hues on a dark, low-saturation background. The hues are roughly red, orange, yellow, light green, dark green, light blue or cyan, dark blue, purple, pink, brown and grey.
  - Magic Sort uses rounded potion flasks with a warm fantasy scene behind the play area.
  - Treat both as hypotheses to check against current screenshots.
- **Sampling hex values reliably** would mean downloading the store screenshots (or recording gameplay) and sampling the flat interior of each liquid band with an eyedropper, away from glass highlights and shading. That was not possible with the text-only tools used here.

### Gaps
- **Hex values and color counts:** none found for any title. No fan wiki or guide listing the color sets of IEC, Magic Sort or Get Color turned up.
- **Sort It 3D, Ball Sort Puzzle and Color Water Sort Woody:** I found no store page, description or review data for these. The publisher of Sort It 3D could not be confirmed. The search tied "Get Color" to Tripledot, not Lion Studios.
- **Google Play pages:** they returned no content (truncated), so Play descriptions, download counts and "What's new" text are missing.
- **UI accent colors:** buttons, currency and the level counter are not documented for any title.

## 2. Which color pairs do players report as confusing, and do any games offer colorblind modes, patterns or symbols?

### Takeaway
Documented complaints name green vs light blue (IEC Water Sort Puzzle) and orange vs yellow-orange (open-source Water Sort). Players also report that dim screens make colors indistinguishable. Among the top-grossing titles, neither Magic Sort nor IEC Water Sort Puzzle declares any accessibility feature. Colorblind support appears only in smaller titles (a "red-green colorblind mode", a "colour blind" ball skin set, symbols in an open-source clone). One developer's response to a contrast complaint was to let players customize the colors rather than fix the palette.

### Cited Findings
- **IEC Water Sort Puzzle, 2021-05-18 review:** "some of the green and light blue colors are very similar and can sometimes be confusing". The reviewer warns it could be "unplayable for those who are colorblind". They suggest unlocking new colors or patterns "to help differentiate which one is which". No developer response is visible. — [App Store reviews: Water Sort Puzzle (IEC)](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/water-sort-puzzle/id1514542157?see-all=reviews)
- **IEC Water Sort Puzzle, 2022-06-05 review:** at the dimmest screen setting, "I can't tell the difference between the colors". The reviewer adds: "I would love to see a toggle for colorblind use". No developer response is visible. — [App Store reviews: Water Sort Puzzle (IEC)](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/water-sort-puzzle/id1514542157?see-all=reviews)
- **Open-source Water Sort (sidhant947), Issue #50:** "some of the water colors look very similar at first glance". The examples are orange (fire symbol) and yellow/orange (star symbol). The reporter adds: "While there are symbols, I find that you primarily identify them by their shade." The maintainer's fix was player customization, not a new default palette: "I'm not color genius so i leave customisation to players always". It shipped in V1.0.19, and the issue was closed 2026-10-03. — [GitHub issue #50](https://github.com/sidhant947/water-sort/issues/50)
- A follow-up bug, where the color customizer showed wrong or missing icons, was fixed in V1.0.21 (2026-10-15). — [GitHub issue #54 (search summary)](https://github.com/sidhant947/water-sort/issues/54)
- **Water Sort Quest (MOBIRIX):** "Red-green colorblind mode available". The listing does not say what the mode changes (palette swap or symbols). Its App Store accessibility section is still blank. — [App Store: Water Sort Quest](https://apps.apple.com/app/id6462847997)
- **Sorting Ball Quiz:** the listing reportedly supports a red-green colorblind mode. This comes from a search summary only; the page was not fetched. — [App Store: Sorting Ball Quiz](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/sorting-ball-quiz/id6475063759?uo=4)
- **Sort It All:** its ball and item skins reportedly include a "colour blind set" alongside fruits, veggies, sports and xmas sets. Its form (symbols or patterns) is unknown. This comes from a search summary only. — [App Store: Sort It All](https://apps.apple.com/tm/app/sort-it-all/id6444219771)
- **Magic Sort and IEC Water Sort Puzzle:** both App Store accessibility sections say the developer "has not yet indicated which accessibility features this app supports". — [Magic Sort](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/magic-sort/id6499209744); [IEC](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/water-sort-puzzle/id1514542157)
- **Red vs dark pink:** a search summary surfaced a store review saying "the red and dark pink are really cl[ose]" (truncated). Fetching the candidate page (Essendon Studios, id1582157018) showed no reviews, so this quote's source app is **unverified**. — [search result candidate](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1582157018)
- **Magic Sort reviews:** these complain about hidden colors ("can't see what colors are in the tubes") rather than similar hues. No colorblind-specific complaint showed up in the visible review set. — [App Store reviews: Magic Sort!](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/magic-sort/id6499209744?see-all=reviews)

### Inferences
- **The documented confusion pairs** are green vs light blue, orange vs yellow-orange, and red vs dark pink (unverified). All three are neighbours on the hue wheel with similar value. So the fix is to separate **lightness (value)**, not just hue.
- **Symbols alone are not enough** (sidhant947 issue). Players still read shade first, so a symbol overlay is a fallback, not a substitute for a well-spaced palette.
- **Screen brightness:** the dim-screen complaint suggests palettes should be tested at low brightness and with night-shift or blue-light filters on.
- **Competitive gap (inference):** none of the top-grossing titles found here (Magic Sort, IEC, Get Color) advertises colorblind support. Offering an optional symbol or pattern overlay could set a game apart.

### Gaps
- **Reddit:** no threads about colorblindness in water or ball sort games were found; searches returned only store listings.
- **Purple vs pink and two-blues complaints:** I found no sourced complaints specifically about these pairs.
- **Developer responses:** none were visible to the IEC colorblind requests. Whether IEC, Grand Games or Tripledot ever added a colorblind option is undocumented.
- **How Water Sort Quest's mode works:** what its red-green mode actually changes is unknown.

## 3. How do these games keep the background from competing with the liquids?

### Takeaway
No source documents the background treatment (value, saturation, blur, vignette, gradient direction) for any leading title. The only sourced facts are that backgrounds are swappable skins and that themed "environments" are a common update item. Everything else here is design inference.

### Cited Findings
- **Swappable backgrounds:**
  - IEC sells backgrounds and bottles for coins; one review says the starting set "aren't bad". — [App Store reviews: IEC](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/water-sort-puzzle/id1514542157?see-all=reviews)
  - Get Color has "wallpapers" as unlockables. — [App Store reviews: Get Color](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/water-sort-puzzle-get-color/id1569776448?see-all=reviews)
  - Water Sort Quest lets players customize backgrounds. — [App Store: Water Sort Quest](https://apps.apple.com/app/id6462847997)
  - Essendon added "new themes and environments". — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1582157018)
- **Seasonal re-skins:** IEC ran a Christmas event update (v20.0.0, Dec 2025). — [App Store: IEC](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/water-sort-puzzle/id1514542157)

### Inferences
- **The constraint skins impose:** if backgrounds are player-chosen skins, the liquid palette must stay legible on every one. The standard way to guarantee that is to keep backgrounds low in saturation and contrast in the area behind the tubes, and to give the tubes their own separation: a glass outline, a rim highlight, or a soft dark backing.
- **Unverified recollection:** the IEC default is a dark, near-uniform background. Magic Sort uses illustrated fantasy scenes framed around a calmer central play area. Verify both against current screenshots before relying on them.

### Gaps
- **No measurements:** I found no documentation or analyses of background value or saturation, blur, vignette or gradient direction for any title.
- **Light vs dark default:** the default for each title is unconfirmed.

## 4. What feedback colors are used for selected tube, invalid move, completed tube and level complete?

### Takeaway
The only documented feedback element is the completion cap or stopper. Water Sort Quest added a "completion effect for stoppers" in v1.0.5. No source documents the colors used for selection, invalid-move or level-complete VFX in any leading title.

### Cited Findings
- **Completion stopper:** Water Sort Quest v1.0.5 "Added completion effect for stoppers", and stoppers are a customizable skin. — [App Store: Water Sort Quest](https://apps.apple.com/app/id6462847997)
- **"Vivid Splashes":** Magic Sort sells pours as "Vivid Splashes", but does not describe the splash or celebration VFX. — [App Store: Magic Sort!](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/magic-sort/id6499209744)

### Inferences
- **Solved-state convention:** capping or stoppering a solved tube appears to be the genre's solved-state signal (Water Sort Quest documents it). That matches this project's recent "cap that snaps on solved tubes" work.
- **Unverified recollection of common patterns:**
  - The selected tube lifts up rather than changing color.
  - An invalid pour gives a shake or wobble rather than a red flash.
  - Level complete triggers multicolor confetti.
  - None of this was sourced in this session.

### Gaps
- **Feedback colors:** none sourced for selection highlight, invalid move, tube complete or level-complete confetti in Magic Sort, IEC, Get Color or any ball sort title. Capturing them would need gameplay video frame sampling.

### Takeaway

### Inferences
